The next morning, Arya left home earlier than usual.

His bag was on his shoulder. His uniform was neat. From the outside, everything looked normal.

But today, he wasn’t going to school.

He walked past the usual turn without hesitation, his pace steady, as if he had already made the decision long ago. At the bus stop, he stood quietly among office-goers and vendors, his eyes fixed ahead but his mind racing.

Behind Alpha Hospital.

That was all he remembered.

The bus arrived with a screech. Arya climbed in, took a window seat, and held onto the metal bar as the city slowly began to change around him. Familiar streets faded into crowded junctions, then into wider roads lined with glass buildings and expensive storefronts.

He kept replaying the same thought.

Why would anyone put property in my name?

The bus dropped him at the depot.

The air felt heavier here; louder, busier, unfamiliar.

“Cycle rickshaw!” a voice called out.

Arya stepped in.

“Alpha Hospital.”

The rickshaw puller nodded and began moving through the chaotic traffic, weaving between autos and bikes. Arya watched everything carefully the roads, the signboards, the turns as if memorizing it all for later. The rickshaw took him just outskirts of city.

When they finally stopped, Arya stepped down slowly.

And then he saw it.

Alpha Hospital.

It towered over everything nearby; polished glass, spotless white walls, guarded gates. It didn’t feel like a hospital. It felt like a place meant only for people who could afford to never worry.

As Arya stood there, trying to take it in, a sudden rush of wind brushed past him.

A car.

Low. Sleek. Expensive.

It moved fast, but not fast enough.

For a fraction of a second, he saw the face inside.

Arya’s body went still.

It was him. The same man.

The one who had threatened him.

Before Arya could react, the car passed through the gates and disappeared inside.

His heartbeat picked up.

Why is he here?

Arya forced himself to move.

He didn’t go through the front gate. Instead, he walked along the outer boundary of the hospital, keeping his distance, his head slightly lowered. Every now and then, he glanced back, as if expecting someone to be watching him.

The building stretched longer than he expected.

Finally, he reached the backside.

And then he stopped. Completely.

Behind the hospital was an open expanse of land.

Not small. Not enclosed. Endless.

Dry soil stretched out toward the horizon, broken only by patches of grass and distant construction debris. No buildings. No fences. Just emptiness.

Arya took a few steps forward, as if the ground itself might disappear if he didn’t confirm it.

This… is mine?

The thought didn’t fit.

Didn’t settle. Didn’t make sense.

He tried to imagine it, his name attached to something this massive.

He couldn’t.

If this is real… then someone chose me.

But why?

The journey back felt longer.

He reached home by evening, his face calm, his thoughts anything but settled.

“Why so late?” his mother asked.

“Sports practice,” Arya replied without hesitation.

A simple lie.

That night, curiosity turned into restlessness.

Arya waited until the house was quiet.

Then he moved.

The almirah stood in the corner of the room. He opened it slowly, careful not to make noise. Inside were files, neatly stacked. He took out his father’s bank passbooks and began flipping through them under dim light.

Numbers. Deposits. Withdrawals. Normal patterns. Nothing unusual.

Nothing that could explain land worth more than everything his family owned combined.

Arya closed the passbooks.

No answers at home.

The next morning, the world itself seemed to shift.

Every news channel said the same thing.

Market crash.

Stocks collapsing. Companies losing value overnight. Some disappearing entirely.

At school, teachers spoke in hushed tones.

“…entire portfolios wiped out…”

“…this hasn’t happened in years…”

Students didn’t care.

Arya did.

Numbers always made sense to him.

People didn’t.

He walked up to his maths teacher after class.

“Sir… what exactly happens during a market crash?”

The teacher looked at him, he knew Arya would ask this.

Then he began explaining.

Liquidity. Panic selling. Chain reactions.

Arya listened carefully, absorbing every word.

Because somewhere deep inside, he felt it this was connected.

Days passed. Then weeks. A full month.

No threats. No men following him.

No strange encounters.

The silence itself felt unnatural.

Arya noticed it.

The crash changed something.

One afternoon, while walking back from school,

A black car pulled up beside him.

Sudden. Silent.

Every window tinted.

Arya froze.

Something about it felt wrong.

Before he could step back, one of the windows rolled down just slightly.

Not enough to see inside.

Just enough.

A hand came out.

And dropped a file onto the road.

No words. No warning.

The window rolled up.

And the car vanished into traffic.

Arya stood there, unmoving.

For a few seconds, the world around him blurred.

Then slowly, he bent down and picked up the file.

It felt heavier than it should have.

He didn’t go home.

Instead, he ran.

Past shops. Past people.

Until he reached a small park.

He sat on a bench, slightly hidden by trees, and opened the file.

Papers. Stamped. Registered. Official.

His eyes scanned rapidly.

Survey numbers. Location details. Legal descriptions.

And then,

His name.

Clear.

Undeniable.

Arya’s breathing became uneven.

He flipped through the pages again.

And again.

Looking for something.

Anything.

That explained it.

A signature. A clue. A connection.

But the more he read, the less he understood.

One thing was certain.

This wasn’t a mistake. This was deliberate.

Arya leaned back on the bench, staring at the file in his hands.

The wind rustled the pages slightly.

For the first time, fear replaced curiosity.

Because now the question had changed.

Not what is happening?

But who is controlling this… and what do they want from him?

Who gave this to him… and why?
